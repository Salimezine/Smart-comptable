The
Prix Bordin
(
French pronunciation:
[
pʁi
bɔʁdɛ̃
]
) is a series of prizes awarded annually by each of the five institutions making up the
Institut Français
since 1835.
History
[
edit
]
The prize was created by
Charles-Laurent Bordin
, a notary in Paris from 1794 to 1820,
[
1
]
who bequeathed 12,000 Francs to the
Institut de France
in his testament dated April 7, 1835, for the foundation of an annual prize to be given to each of the five:
the
Académie Française
, in order to "encourage high literature"; the prize was given irregularly until 1988
[
2
]
the
Académie des inscriptions et belles-lettres
[
3
]
the
French Academy of Sciences
. The awarding of the prize is linked with a contest organized by the Academy.
[
4
]
the
Académie des Sciences morales et politiques
: the biennial prize is awarded to "works treating upon subjects relating to the public interest, to the wellbeing of humanity, to the progress of science and to national honor (of France)."
[
5
]
the
Académie des Beaux-Arts
: to reward works on painting, sculpture, architecture, engraving or music.

citation needed
]
